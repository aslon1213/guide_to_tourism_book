Imperatives — giving directions and instructions

When do we use it? We use the imperative (the base form of the verb, with no subject) to give directions, instructions and friendly advice. It sounds clear, not rude — especially with please or a smile!

  • Turn left at the fountain and go straight ahead.
  • Take the second turning on your right.
  • Don't take the first street — it's a dead end.
  • Please follow the signposts to the bazaar.

Prepositions of place — saying exactly where something is

When do we use it? Prepositions of place tell a visitor the exact position of a building, usually after is/are or you'll see. They turn a street name into a picture the listener can follow.

  • The information centre is opposite the railway station.
  • The café is between the bank and the pharmacy.
  • The ticket office is next to the main entrance, on the corner of Silk Street.
  • There is a taxi rank in front of the hotel, and the car park is behind it.

Useful language — asking for and giving clarification

Asking someone to repeat or slow down
  • Sorry, could you say that again, please?
  • Could you speak a little more slowly, please?
  • I didn't quite catch the street name.
Checking you understand
  • Did you say turn left at the fountain?
  • So, the bazaar is opposite the mosque — is that right?
  • Let me check: first right, then second left?
Clarifying what you said
  • I mean the second street, not the first.
  • In other words, keep the river on your left.
  • What I'm saying is, it's only a five-minute walk.
Confirming or correcting
  • That's right. / Exactly.
  • Not quite — turn right, not left.

Rule of use: repeat the key words back to the speaker and confirm before you set off — it is polite and saves you a long walk in the wrong direction!
