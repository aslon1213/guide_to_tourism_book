SPEAKING
15. Work in pairs. Role-play the situations below. Then swap roles.
Student A — Guest

You are looking for accommodation. Read your situation, ask about rooms, facilities and prices. Compare the options and make a decision.

Student B — Receptionist

You work at the Silk Road Plaza Hotel. Describe your rooms and facilities with there is / there are and has got, answer questions and recommend the best option.

Situation 1

A family of four from Seoul needs two connecting rooms with breakfast. Their budget is $100 a night in total.

Situation 2

A backpacker from Brazil wants the cheapest bed in town and asks what facilities the hostel floor has got.

Situation 3

A honeymoon couple from Cairo wants the most romantic suite in the hotel, with a view and room service.

Situation 4

A business traveller from Berlin needs the quietest room available, with fast Wi-Fi and a desk, near the conference hall.

16. Describe and guess. Describe one of the words below with there is / there are, has got and adjectives — but don't say the word! Your partner guesses.
  • hostel
  • resort
  • suite
  • lobby
  • B&B
  • concierge
  • room service
  • twin room

Example: “This place is cheaper than a hotel. There are bunk beds in big shared rooms, and it has got a shared kitchen…” — “A hostel!”

WRITING
17. Complete the sentences with your own ideas.
  1. The most famous hotel in my country is .
  2. A good hostel has always got .
  3. In my dream hotel room, there is .
  4. A guesthouse is cosier than .
  5. For me, the most important facility is .
  6. My city has got .
  7. Resorts are usually more expensive than .
  8. There aren't any in a typical hostel.
  9. The best place I have ever stayed was .
  10. A suite is more spacious than .
18. Write a short paragraph (60–100 words) comparing two places to stay in your town or two famous hotels. Use there is / there are, has got, at least three adjectives and three comparative or superlative forms.
