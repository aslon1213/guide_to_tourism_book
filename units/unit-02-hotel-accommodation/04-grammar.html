Adjectives — describing places

When do we use them? Adjectives tell us what a place is like. They go before a noun (a spacious suite) or after be / look / seem (The lobby is luxurious). Adjectives never change for plural: two cosy rooms, not cosies.

  • The guesthouse is cosy and quiet.
  • There is a spacious lobby with comfortable sofas.
  • Our resort has got a modern gym and a luxurious spa.
  • The hostel looks affordable but a little noisy.

There is / There are — saying what exists

When do we use it? To say that something exists in a place — perfect for describing hotel facilities. Use there is with singular nouns and there are with plurals.

  • There is a 24-hour reception in the lobby.
  • There are two restaurants on the ground floor.
  • There isn't a swimming pool, but there is a sauna.
  • Are there any family rooms available this weekend?

Have got / Has got — talking about features

When do we use it? To talk about possession and features — what a hotel, a room or a person owns or includes. In British English, have got / has got means the same as have / has.

  • Every room has got air conditioning and free Wi-Fi.
  • We've got a rooftop terrace with views of the old town.
  • The hostel hasn't got a restaurant, but it has got a shared kitchen.
  • Has your room got a sea view?

Comparatives and superlatives — comparing places to stay

When do we use them? Use the comparative + than to compare two places. Use the + superlative to say one place is number one in a group. Short adjectives take -er / -est; adjectives ending in -y change to -ier / -iest; long adjectives use more / the most.

  • A hostel is cheaper than a boutique hotel.
  • The suite is more spacious than the double room.
  • The Emerald Resort is the most luxurious hotel in the region.
  • Their breakfast is good, but ours is better — in fact, it's the best in town!
