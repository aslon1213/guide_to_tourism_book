GRAMMAR

will — decisions, offers, promises and predictions

We use will ('ll) when we decide something at the moment of speaking, when we offer or promise to do something, and for predictions about the future (often with I think / probably).

  • One moment, please — I'll check availability for those dates.
  • Don't worry, we won't charge your card before May.
  • I think the Bukhara tour will sell out quickly in the peak season.
  • I promise I'll send the invoice this afternoon.

be going to — plans and intentions

We use be going to for plans and intentions we decided before the moment of speaking.

  • We're going to visit Samarkand in May, so I'd like to book three nights.
  • Sara isn't going to pay the full amount today — only the deposit.
  • Are you going to travel in the low season?

Present Continuous — fixed arrangements

We use the Present Continuous for fixed future arrangements with other people — bookings, meetings and timetables in a diary. There is usually a date or a time in the sentence.

  • Mr Tanaka is arriving on 12 June at 14:30.
  • We're meeting the tour group at the airport on Friday.
  • The guests aren't staying all week — they're checking out on Sunday.
