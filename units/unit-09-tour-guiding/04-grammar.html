Useful language — sequencing a tour

Starting
  • First, / First of all, we'll visit the old town.
  • Our first stop is the city museum.
  • To begin with, let me show you the main gate.
Continuing
  • Then we'll walk to the bazaar.
  • Next, we'll cross the bridge.
  • After that, we'll stop for tea.
  • While you're taking photos, I'll buy the tickets.
Finishing
  • Finally, we'll return to the hotel.
  • At the end of the tour, you'll have free time.
  • Last but not least, don't miss the sunset view!

Rule of use: sequencing markers usually come at the beginning of a sentence and are followed by a comma. They help your audience follow the plan.

Past Simple and Past Continuous — telling stories about places

When do we use them? We use the Past Simple for finished actions and the main events of a story. We use the Past Continuous for longer background actions that were in progress at a past moment. They often work together with when and while.

  • The emir built this summer palace in 1911. (finished event — Past Simple)
  • Merchants were selling silk on this square when the great fire started.
  • While we were walking to the minaret, a tourist asked me about the legend.
  • The group wasn't listening — everyone was photographing the storks on the dome.

Useful language — engaging your audience

Rhetorical questions (creating curiosity)
  • Have you ever wondered why this tower has no windows?
  • Can you guess how old these walls are?
  • What do you think happened next?
Inviting attention
  • Take a closer look at the blue tiles above the gate.
  • If you look to your left, you'll see the city walls.
  • Notice the carved wooden door — most visitors walk straight past it.
Checking and involving the group
  • Can everybody hear me at the back?
  • Is everyone with me? Great — let's move on.
  • Does anyone know what this building was used for?
  • Feel free to ask questions at any time.

Rule of use: a good guide doesn't only give facts — questions and invitations keep listeners curious, awake and involved.
