Imperatives — giving safety instructions

When do we use it? We use the imperative (the base verb, no subject) to give direct orders, instructions and warnings. In safety notices and training manuals, the full form Do not sounds more formal and serious than Don't.

  • Keep calm and walk to the nearest emergency exit.
  • Do not use the lifts during a fire.
  • Always check the first-aid kit at the start of your shift.
  • Never block a fire door with luggage trolleys.
FormExample
AffirmativeLeave the building. / Call an ambulance.
NegativeDo not (Don't) touch the fire blanket without training.
With always / neverAlways keep exits clear. / Never re-enter a burning building.

First Conditional — giving warnings

When do we use it? We use if + Present Simple in one clause and will + base verb in the other to warn people about real future results. The imperative often replaces will in the result clause of a safety warning.

  • If you hear the alarm, you will see green exit signs — follow them.
  • If you block an emergency exit, you will put lives in danger.
  • Guests will panic if staff do not stay calm.
If-clause (Present Simple)Result clause (will + verb)
AffirmativeIf the alarm sounds,the lifts will stop automatically.
NegativeIf staff do not stay calm,guests will not follow instructions.
QuestionIf a guest faints,what will you do?

Past Simple — reporting incidents

When do we use it? An incident report describes finished past events in the order they happened. Formal reports prefer verbs such as occur, respond, injure, evacuate and exact times and places.

  • The accident occurred at 14:20 near the pool bar.
  • A waiter slipped on the wet floor and injured his wrist.
  • Security responded immediately and called an ambulance.
  • Staff did not move the guest until the paramedics arrived.
