Useful language — formal vs informal register

Rule of use: with business clients, in emails and at events, choose the formal version; keep informal language for friends and colleagues you know well.

Greeting someone
  • Formal: Dear Ms Osei, / Dear Sir or Madam,  →  Informal: Hi Amara! / Hey!
Making a request
  • Formal: Would you be able to confirm the dates? / Could you please send the agenda?
  • Informal: Can you check the dates? / Send me the agenda, OK?
Saying no politely
  • Formal: I am afraid that is not possible. / Unfortunately, we are unable to offer a discount.
  • Informal: Sorry, no can do. / We can't do that.
Thanking and closing
  • Formal: Thank you for your assistance. / Yours sincerely, / Kind regards,
  • Informal: Thanks a lot! / Cheers! / See you!

Present Continuous — fixed plans on a business schedule

When do we use it? When a future event is already arranged and on the programme — the time, place and people are fixed. In business English it makes a schedule sound certain and professional.

  • The keynote speaker is arriving at 9:00 tomorrow.
  • We are hosting a trade fair for 200 exhibitors in May.
  • Our drivers are meeting all delegates at the airport on Monday.
  • Is the catering team preparing a vegetarian menu for the gala dinner?

Useful language — the professional email frame

Rule of use: a professional email always follows the same frame — greeting → purpose → details/requests → attachment → polite close.

Stating your purpose
  • I am writing to enquire about… / I am writing with regard to…
  • Further to our telephone conversation, …
Requesting and confirming
  • Could you please confirm availability for 9–10 April?
  • We would appreciate it if you could send a detailed quotation.
  • I am pleased to confirm your booking.
Attaching documents
  • Please find attached the agenda / our sample menu.
Closing
  • I look forward to hearing from you.
  • Yours sincerely, (you know the name) / Yours faithfully, (Dear Sir or Madam)
From: events@silkroadgrand.uz
