Useful language — polite requests and offers at the front desk

Rule of use: at a hotel desk we soften requests with could / would and make offers with would you like / shall I — direct forms like “Give me your passport” sound rude.

Making polite requests (receptionist)
  • Could I see your passport, please?
  • Could you fill in this registration form, please?
  • Would you mind signing at the bottom?
  • May I have your booking confirmation?
Making polite requests (guest)
  • Could I have a late check-out, please?
  • Could you call me a taxi, please?
Making offers
  • Would you like some help with your luggage?
  • Shall I arrange a wake-up call for you?
  • Can I offer you a welcome drink?
Responding politely
  • Certainly, madam. / Of course, sir.
  • Here you are.
  • Yes, please. / No, thank you.

Present Perfect — talking about recent actions

When do we use it? We use the Present Perfect (have / has + past participle) for recent actions that are connected to now — very common at check-in and check-out. We often add just (a moment ago), already (sooner than expected) and yet (in negatives and questions).

  • Mr. Tanaka has just arrived from Tokyo.
  • I've already signed the registration form.
  • The porter hasn't brought your luggage yet.
  • Have you packed your suitcase yet? Check-out is at noon.
