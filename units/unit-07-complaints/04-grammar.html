Useful language — apologizing and responding to complaints

Rule of use: apologize first, show you understand, then offer a solution — in that order.

Apologizing
  • I'm so sorry about that.
  • I do apologize for the inconvenience.
  • Please accept our sincere apologies.
  • We're terribly sorry this happened.
Showing empathy
  • I completely understand.
  • That must have been very frustrating.
  • I can see why you're upset.
Promising action
  • I'll sort it out right away.
  • Let me see what I can do.
  • We'll send someone up immediately.
  • We'll make sure it doesn't happen again.

Past Simple — describing what went wrong

When do we use it? When a guest complains, they describe finished events in the past: what happened, when it happened, and what the staff did (or didn't do). That is the Past Simple.

  • The shuttle bus arrived forty minutes late.
  • We didn't receive the towels we asked for.
  • Did the porter bring your luggage to the room?
  • I booked a double room, but you gave us a twin.
AffirmativeNegativeQuestion
regular (complain)The guest complained.The guest didn't complain.Did the guest complain?
irregular (lose)The airline lost my bag.The airline didn't lose my bag.Did the airline lose my bag?

could / would / might — offering polite solutions

When do we use them? Hotel and restaurant staff soften their offers and suggestions with modal verbs. They sound calmer and more professional than direct orders.

  • could → a possible option or offer: We could move you to a quieter room. / Could I offer you a drink while you wait?
  • would → polite offers and wishes: Would you like an extra blanket? / We would be happy to upgrade you.
  • might → a careful, gentle suggestion: You might prefer a room on the top floor — it's much quieter.
