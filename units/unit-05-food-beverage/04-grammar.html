Countable and uncountable nouns — talking about food and drink

When do we use it? Countable nouns are things we can count one by one (a menu, two starters, three desserts). Uncountable nouns are things we cannot count as separate items (rice, water, bread, advice) — they have no plural form. We use many / a few with countable nouns, much / a little with uncountable nouns, and some / any with both. To count uncountable food, use a container or portion: a glass of water, a bowl of soup, a piece of cake.

  • We serve many desserts, but we don't have much ice cream today.
  • Would you like some bread with your soup?
  • Could I have a glass of water and two cups of green tea?
  • There isn't much rice left at the buffet.

Useful language — making polite suggestions to guests

Rule of use: soften suggestions with question forms and modal verbs — direct orders like “Take the soup” sound rude to guests.

Recommending a dish
  • May I recommend the grilled trout?
  • Why don't you try our chef's special?
  • I'd suggest the lamb kebab — it's very popular with our guests.
Offering more
  • Would you like some more tea?
  • Shall I refill your glass?
  • Can I get you anything else?
Suggesting alternatives
  • How about a vegetarian plov instead?
  • If you prefer something lighter, you could try the green salad.
  • Perhaps you'd like to see our dessert menu?

First Conditional — real possibilities in food service

When do we use it? We use the First Conditional to talk about a real or likely situation in the future and its result. Form: If + Present Simple, … will + base verb. The if-clause can also come second — then we don't need a comma.

  • If a guest has an allergy, the waiter will inform the chef.
  • If you book before 6 p.m., we will reserve a window table for you.
  • You won't wait long if you choose the buffet.
  • What will you do if a guest doesn't like the dish?
