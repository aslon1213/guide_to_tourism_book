GLOSSARY

This glossary brings together every key word and phrase taught in the fourteen units of A Complete Guide to Tourism English. Entries are listed alphabetically; each one shows the word, its pronunciation in the International Phonetic Alphabet, its part of speech (n. noun, v. verb, adj. adjective, phr. phrase), a simple definition, an example sentence from a tourism context, and the unit where the word first appears. Use it to revise before tests, to check a meaning while you work through an activity, or to return to the unit where a word is practised in full.

accommodation /əˌkɒməˈdeɪʃn/ n. a place to stay, such as a hotel, hostel or guesthouse. e.g. The tour price includes accommodation and breakfast. (Unit 1)
adventure tourism /ədˈventʃə ˈtʊərɪzəm/ n. tourism with exciting outdoor activities such as climbing or rafting. e.g. Adventure tourism takes visitors into the mountains and the desert. (Unit 1)
affordable /əˈfɔːdəbl/ adj. cheap enough for most people to pay for. e.g. Hostels are an affordable choice for young travellers. (Unit 2)
agenda /əˈdʒendə/ n. the list of topics or sessions planned for a meeting or conference. e.g. Please find attached the agenda for Friday's seminar. (Unit 13)
all-inclusive /ˌɔːl ɪnˈkluːsɪv/ adj. with meals, drinks and most activities included in one single price. e.g. Their all-inclusive package covers every meal and two guided excursions. (Unit 12)
allergy /ˈælədʒi/ n. a medical reaction to a food such as nuts, milk or eggs. e.g. One guest had a serious nut allergy, so we informed the chef. (Unit 5)
amenities /əˈmiːnətiz/ n. useful or pleasant extras that make a stay more comfortable, such as Wi-Fi or toiletries. e.g. Free Wi-Fi and air conditioning are standard amenities here. (Unit 2)
ancient /ˈeɪnʃənt/ adj. extremely old; from the distant past. e.g. Tourists wander through the ancient streets of the walled city. (Unit 10)
anecdote /ˈænɪkdəʊt/ n. a short, amusing true story about a real person or event. e.g. He told a funny anecdote about a sultan and his cook. (Unit 9)
apologize /əˈpɒlədʒaɪz/ v. to say that you are sorry for a problem or mistake. e.g. The receptionist apologized for the noisy neighbours. (Unit 7)
archway /ˈɑːtʃweɪ/ n. an entrance or passage under a curved arch. e.g. We passed through a tall stone archway into the inner city. (Unit 10)
assembly point /əˈsembli pɔɪnt/ n. a safe place outside a building where everyone must gather during an evacuation. e.g. Our assembly point is the car park opposite the main entrance. (Unit 14)
attract /əˈtrækt/ v. to make people want to come to a place or use a service. e.g. The new night market attracts thousands of visitors every weekend. (Unit 12)
audience /ˈɔːdiəns/ n. the group of people who are listening to a speaker. e.g. A good guide watches the audience and notices when they are tired. (Unit 9)
availability /əˌveɪləˈbɪləti/ n. the rooms, seats or places that are still free to book. e.g. Let me check availability for the tenth of July. (Unit 3)
award-winning /əˈwɔːd wɪnɪŋ/ adj. having won one or more prizes for being excellent. e.g. Our award-winning spa was named the best in the region last year. (Unit 12)
B&B (bed and breakfast) /ˌbiː ən ˈbiː/ n. a small private house or guesthouse that offers a room for the night and breakfast in the morning. e.g. We stayed at a friendly B&B near the lake. (Unit 2)
bargain /ˈbɑːɡən/ v. to discuss a price with a seller in order to pay less. e.g. At Siyob Bazaar you can bargain politely over the price of dried fruit. (Unit 11)
bazaar /bəˈzɑː(r)/ n. a large market, especially in Asia, where many traders sell goods. e.g. You can buy silk scarves and fresh spices at the bazaar. (Unit 6)
between /bɪˈtwiːn/ phr. in the space that separates two places. e.g. The information centre is between the bank and the post office. (Unit 6)
beverage /ˈbevərɪdʒ/ n. any kind of drink, hot or cold. e.g. Hot beverages such as tea and coffee are free at breakfast. (Unit 5)
book /bʊk/ v. to reserve a room, ticket or tour in advance. e.g. We booked three nights at the Emir Hotel. (Unit 3)
book in advance /bʊk ɪn ədˈvɑːns/ phr. to reserve something early, before the date you need it. e.g. In peak season you should book in advance to get the best rooms. (Unit 12)
booking /ˈbʊkɪŋ/ n. a reservation for a room, a ticket or a tour. e.g. The receptionist takes bookings by phone and online. (Unit 1)
booking reference /ˈbʊkɪŋ ˈrefrəns/ n. the letters and numbers that identify a reservation. e.g. Your booking reference is NT-4172. (Unit 3)
bow /baʊ/ v. to bend your head or body forward to greet somebody or show respect. e.g. The guide taught us to bow slightly when we met the elders of the village. (Unit 11)
breakout room /ˈbreɪkaʊt ruːm/ n. a smaller room used for group sessions away from the main conference hall. e.g. Workshop B is taking place in the largest breakout room. (Unit 13)
breathtaking /ˈbreθteɪkɪŋ/ adj. extremely beautiful or impressive, almost making you stop breathing. e.g. The view from the watchtower at sunset is absolutely breathtaking. (Unit 10)
bridge /brɪdʒ/ n. a structure that carries a road or path over a river. e.g. Cross the bridge and the park is on your right. (Unit 6)
brochure /ˈbrəʊʃə/ n. a thin magazine with pictures that advertises a product, holiday or place. e.g. She picked up a brochure about Silk Road tours at the travel fair. (Unit 12)
buffet /ˈbʊfeɪ/ n. a meal where guests serve themselves from a long table of dishes. e.g. The breakfast buffet opens at seven o'clock. (Unit 5)
business travel /ˈbɪznəs ˌtrævl/ n. travelling to meetings, conferences and other work events. e.g. Business travel brings many visitors to the capital city. (Unit 1)
call back /ˌkɔːl ˈbæk/ phr. to phone somebody again at a later time. e.g. Mr Lee is out of the office. Could you call back after lunch? (Unit 8)
caller /ˈkɔːlə/ n. the person who makes a telephone call. e.g. Always ask the caller for their name and number. (Unit 8)
campaign /kæmˈpeɪn/ n. a planned series of advertisements with one clear goal. e.g. The airline launched a winter campaign to attract more passengers. (Unit 12)
cancel /ˈkænsl/ v. to say that a planned booking will not happen. e.g. She had to cancel her trip to Bukhara. (Unit 3)
cancellation policy /ˌkænsəˈleɪʃn ˈpɒləsi/ n. the rules about returning money when a guest does not travel. e.g. Read the cancellation policy carefully before you pay. (Unit 3)
catering /ˈkeɪtərɪŋ/ n. the service of providing food and drink at an event. e.g. The catering for the gala dinner includes a full vegetarian menu. (Unit 13)
charge /tʃɑːdʒ/ v. to take money from someone as payment. e.g. We won't charge your card before May. (Unit 3)
check in /ˌtʃek ˈɪn/ v. to arrive at a hotel, give your details and receive your room. e.g. You can check in any time after two o'clock. (Unit 4)
check out /ˌtʃek ˈaʊt/ v. to pay your bill and leave the hotel at the end of your stay. e.g. We have to check out before twelve o'clock. (Unit 4)
check-in date /ˈtʃekɪn deɪt/ n. the day a guest arrives and a stay begins. e.g. Choose your check-in date and the number of nights. (Unit 3)
chef /ʃef/ n. a professional cook in a hotel or restaurant kitchen. e.g. The chef cooks plov for two hundred guests every day. (Unit 1)
citadel /ˈsɪtədəl/ n. a fortress that protects a city, usually built on high ground. e.g. The ancient citadel rises on a hill above the river. (Unit 10)
clear /klɪə/ v. to take empty plates and glasses away from a table. e.g. Please clear table six before the next group arrives. (Unit 5)
code word /ˈkəʊd wɜːd/ n. a word such as Alpha or Bravo used to make a letter clear when spelling on the phone. e.g. Use code words when you spell a guest's surname over the phone. (Unit 8)
cold meal /kəʊld miːl/ n. food that is served cold when it should be hot. e.g. The waiter took the cold meal back to the kitchen and apologized. (Unit 7)
commentary /ˈkɒməntri/ n. the spoken information a guide gives during a tour. e.g. Her lively commentary made the bus tour unforgettable. (Unit 9)
compensation /ˌkɒmpenˈseɪʃn/ n. money or gifts given to a customer to make a bad situation better. e.g. As compensation, the airline offered a free flight voucher. (Unit 7)
complain /kəmˈpleɪn/ v. to say that you are not happy or satisfied with something. e.g. Mr Becker complained about the noise at the front desk. (Unit 7)
complaint /kəmˈpleɪnt/ n. a statement that you are not happy with a product or service. e.g. The front desk received a complaint about the leaking tap. (Unit 7)
complimentary /ˌkɒmplɪˈmentri/ adj. given free of charge to a guest or customer. e.g. Every booking includes a complimentary airport transfer. (Unit 12)
concierge /ˌkɒnsiˈeəʒ/ n. a hotel employee who helps guests with tours, taxis, restaurants and tickets. e.g. The concierge booked theatre tickets for the guests. (Unit 2)
conference /ˈkɒnfərəns/ n. a large formal meeting where people with shared professional interests listen to talks and exchange ideas. e.g. Over 300 delegates attended the tourism conference in Tashkent. (Unit 13)
